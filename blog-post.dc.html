How to block in a portrait — value first

The fastest way to make a portrait feel solid is to ignore the features for the first hour. No eyes, no nostrils, no eyelashes — just big shapes and big values. It sounds like a delay. It's actually the shortcut.

Why values come first

A portrait reads from across the room because of its value structure, not its details. If the light side and shadow side are correctly grouped, the likeness survives almost any amount of clumsy drawing. If they're muddled, no amount of careful rendering will save it.

The squint test

Squint at your subject until your eyes water. What survives? Two or three big shapes. That's your painting for the first hour — those shapes and nothing else.

Three values are enough

Mix one light, one halftone, one shadow. Resist the urge to make more. When every part of the head belongs clearly to one of the three, you've earned the right to subdivide.

"If the big shapes are right, the small shapes forgive you."

The block-in, step by step

Big shapes before features

Start with the outer envelope of the head — the tilt of the skull, the angle of the jaw. Check it twice against your reference before touching anything inside it.

Finding the shadow line

Trace the terminator — the line where light turns into shadow — around the whole head as one continuous path. Everything on one side is light; everything on the other is shadow. Keep them grouped.

Tying in the background

Paint the background up to the head early. The edge between head and background is half the likeness — leaving it for last means painting blind all session.

Common mistakes

Detail too early

An eye painted in the first hour is a magnet — you'll keep adjusting everything around it to protect it. Don't give yourself the magnet.

Chalky halftones

Halftones go chalky when you lighten them with white alone. Warm them as you lighten — a touch of yellow ochre keeps the skin alive.

Homework

One head, three values, ninety minutes, no features. Post it in the Discord channel — I look at every block-in students share.
