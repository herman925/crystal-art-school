# Blog articles

**Posts → Add New.** This is the freest part of the site — it is meant to be written in,
not configured.

## The blocks you can use

Paragraph, Heading, Image, List, Quote, Separator, Gallery, Columns, Group and Cover.

Use that working set for the article. Advanced custom formatting and wide tables are not part of these instructions. For information that would otherwise need a table, use a heading with a short list underneath so it remains readable on a phone.

## Photographs in articles

Put the photo above the text it belongs to, rather than beside it. Side-by-side collapses
to stacked on a phone regardless, so putting it above is what readers actually see, and it
is two clicks instead of hunting for a layout block.

## The topic label

Every article carries a topic — the ribbon down the side of the featured card, the small
label on each card in the list, and the row of filter buttons above them.

Choose an existing **Category** in the article editor. Request a new topic from the webmaster; the limited content account does not provide category-management screens.

Two cautions:

**Categories, not Tags.** The editor offers both. Only Categories reach the page; anything
typed into Tags goes nowhere.

**"Uncategorized" is skipped.** WordPress puts every uncategorised article there
automatically, so it is ignored rather than printed as a topic. An article with no real
category simply shows no topic, which is better than showing a WordPress word.

## Headings decide the layout

An article with **Heading** blocks in it is laid out as a guide: a contents list at the top,
and each heading opening its own numbered section. An article with no headings is laid out as
an essay — first paragraph large, then straight prose.

There is no setting for this. It follows what you wrote.

For a numbered guide, use a **List** block and set it to numbered. The numbers carry on
across sections if you set the **Start value** on each list, so a guide can run 1 to 21 across
six sections without you typing a single number.

## Photographs in an article

The main photograph is the **Featured image**. It is used in four different places at four
different shapes — a very wide banner at the top of the article, a card in the list, a small
tile at the bottom of other articles, and the large featured card. All four crop what you
give them.

The banner is the extreme one: it is over five times wider than it is tall. A portrait
photograph put there loses almost everything except a strip through the middle. Pick
something wide, or something where the middle band carries the picture.

## The reading time

Worked out from the length of the article. You cannot type it, and it corrects itself when
you edit.

It does not yet count Chinese correctly — a Chinese article reads "1 min" whatever its
length. The webmaster knows.

## Articles on the homepage

The homepage carries a row of your **six newest articles**. Nothing to set up — but an
article with no **Featured image** is left out of it, because the card is mostly picture.

Beside that row is a dark **TOOLS** square. It leads to your articles filed under the topic
**Tools**, so give one article that category before pointing anyone at it.

## The introduction on the blog page

The prepared Blog editing page awaits installation, checks and handover. Once available,
use the Blog card in **Content hub** and choose its introduction link. Save the introduction
with its **Save** button. Empty it and the paragraph disappears. The related links open the
separate article list; the saved-page link opens the public Blog in another tab.

## Your three existing articles

The supplied articles may carry a note listing proposed corrections, such as brand spellings or changes to wording. Check those changes before publishing. Clear the note only if you approve them; otherwise ask the webmaster to resolve the wording first. Do not publish an article while an unresolved editing note is visible.

The note is a box on the right of the editor called **Developer's note**. Clear it and press
Update, and the amber panel disappears from the article. Nothing else to undo.

The **Posts** list has a **Dev note** column that reads "Showing on the article" whenever one
is live, so a note cannot go out unnoticed.
